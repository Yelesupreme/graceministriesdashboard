# PROBLEM STATEMENT
Grace Flow Ministries, a large-scale religious organization with 100,000 registered members, lacked structured visibility into its membership demographics.

As a Junior Data Analyst, My responsibility is to:
- Provide a statutory report using LOOKERSTUDIO to help leadership understand who their members are.
- How members were reached
- Where financial and pastoral gaps exist in order to make informed decisions on growth strategy, discipleship programmes, and resource allocation.

# INSIGHTS
- 100,000 total members — 41,887 tithers (41.9%) vs 58,113 non-tithers (58.1%)
- 65.2% of members are Single; the dominant demographic by marital status
- Youth dominate attendance as 20–29 age group leads at 44,929; engagement drops sharply after 30
- Word of Mouth is the #1 acquisition channel at 40,048 which is nearly double Social Media (24,993)
- 55,064 members completed Believer's School (55%) but 44,936 have not
- Female members slightly outnumber males — 52.2% vs 47.8%, but male tithers appear marginally higher

# RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Launch a Targeted Tithing Activation Campaign 58% of members are not tithing. Rather than a blanket campaign, segment by demographics e.g single young adults (20–29) who dominate membership are likely in early career stages. Design tithe education and commitment drives specifically for this group, framing giving within their financial reality.

2. Address the 30+ Drop-Off Before It Becomes a Retention Crisis Membership drops from 44,929 (age 20–29) to 15,034 (age 30–39) — a 66% fall. This is a critical leak in the pipeline. Members in their 30s are typically entering marriage, parenthood, and career demands. Dedicated programmes for this life stage (young families, career mentorship, midweek services) could significantly improve retention.

3. Formalise and Reward the Word-of-Mouth Channel 40,048 members came through Word of Mouth — the single most powerful acquisition channel. This is happening organically with no structured programme behind it. A formal referral or ambassador initiative could double its impact with minimal investment.

4. Invest More in Social Media — It's Already Working as Social Media brought in 24,993 members, making it the second largest channel. Flyers (9,997), Invitations (7,954), and Online (6,919) trail significantly. Reallocating budget from print/flyer spend toward social content and digital outreach is a clear efficiency gain.

5. Convert the 44,936 Believer's School Non-Completers 45% of members have not completed Believer's School. Incomplete discipleship correlates with lower engagement and likely lower tithing rates. A re-enrolment drive with flexible scheduling (evening/weekend cohorts) targeting this group should be a pastoral priority.

6. Build a Singles Ministry with Financial Discipleship at the Core 65.2% of members are single. There is no demographic more reachable for financial stewardship teaching than young, single adults who are forming financial habits now. A dedicated singles ministry with tithing and generosity as a pillar could meaningfully shift the 58% non-tither rate over 12–18 months.

# Risks to Watch

- The 30–49 age groups are severely underrepresented — the church risks becoming a youth revolving door rather than a multigenerational community

- A 58% non-tither rate limits the financial sustainability of a 100,000-member organisation

- Heavy reliance on Word of Mouth as the #1 channel is uncontrollable and unscalable without formalisation

- 45% Believer's School incompletion signals a discipleship gap that may widen over time
